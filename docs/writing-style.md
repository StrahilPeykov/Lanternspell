# Lanternspell writing and world specificity

Write for the player's current action. Bellweather is a working college with equipment, deadlines, repairs and people with differing priorities. Magic matters more when ordinary things are allowed to remain ordinary.

## Three registers

- **System/UI:** short, concrete, actionable. Say “Victory”, “Restore 12 health”, “Waiting for your friend”, “Settings”. A confirmation button names the action. Mechanical information uses the same names as the cards. No poetry in errors, costs, tutorials or save controls.
- **NPC:** give each speaker a practical concern and a vocabulary. Iona maintains the observatory and lends equipment; she can be brisk, dry or warm. She does not narrate the game's architecture. One concrete request usually suffices.
- **Lore/exceptional magic:** permit an unusual image when the moment earns it. Keep it sparse. Do not personify every lamp, book and building or make each line an aphorism.

For substantial flavor, ask: could twenty unrelated fantasy games use this unchanged? Does it reveal a specific fact, desire, action or joke? Would deletion improve the scene? Does this moment need poetry? Prefer deletion to an interchangeable flourish. Avoid repetitive memory/light/dream/weaving abstractions, sentimental conclusions, ornamental titles and habitual “a little” diminutives. This is an editorial test, not a banned-word list.

## Onboarding and visual writing

Let players act before explaining the next system. One temporary hint; remove it when the action is demonstrated. Put long rules in help. Use visible consequences and short labels beside the relevant choice. Do not repeat a direction in dialogue, a note, a toast and the objective.

Give spaces practical identities: equipment stores, teaching rooms, repairs, notices, work surfaces. Prefer a few specific traces of use to dense thematic decoration. Keep ordinary surfaces quiet. Review runtime screenshots as well as strings.

## Diagnostic references (verified 11 September 2026)

- [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) explicitly describes observations rather than prescriptive rules. Its warnings about interchangeable emphasis and generic language are useful diagnostics; do not use it to evade detection or mechanically ban punctuation.
- [Roblox: Onboarding techniques](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding-techniques) discusses visual prominence, highlights and contextual hints, and the risk of lengthy prescriptive tutorials. Apply those principles through play rather than copying a tutorial template.
- [inkle: Pacing dialogue](https://medium.com/@inklestudios/pacing-dialogue-5bd5a99bfc20) discusses rhythm, subtext and short lines instead of block speeches. The indexed excerpt was readable; the full page did not render through the research tool. Treat this as a narrow reference, not a fully reviewed manual.

Review all runtime surfaces before shipping: title, dialogue, discoveries, prompts, spells, intentions, outcomes, toasts, multiplayer, settings and help. Read them in context; concise copy can still be badly timed.
